# Research Status and Plan

## Answer to the current research question

Two source-specific observational analyses were completed and kept separate. The component–motion route found held-out encoding in the eligible animals. Its annotation-related evidence was heterogeneous, and adding annotation-related predictors did not consistently improve on motion-related predictors. The separate observational prediction route was non-supportive overall because its held-out improvement relative to the fixed baseline was directionally mixed.

## Completed work

The project completed two pre-specified observational implementations with deterministic tests. The first assessed association between pre-extracted neural components and behavioural predictor families under held-out evaluation. The second used a distinct held-out prediction design. Both have bounded interpretations and are not interchangeable analyses.

## Successful and non-supportive results

The supported result is a limited, source-specific component–motion encoding association. The non-supportive results are the heterogeneous annotation-related findings, the absence of uniform benefit from combined predictors, and the directionally mixed held-out performance in the separate route. These outcomes are observational and predictive only.

## What cannot be concluded

The completed analyses do not establish causality, a biological mechanism, a common neural state across animals, learning, memory, individual-neuron effects, raw-voxel effects, or a cross-system relationship. The two routes are not pooled and neither is a replication or substitute for the other.

## Next research decision

Any future data-dependent analysis must preserve route separation and state its intended inference before results are inspected. Public contributions are welcome for data-free tooling, synthetic tests, documentation, and review of observational-analysis safeguards.
