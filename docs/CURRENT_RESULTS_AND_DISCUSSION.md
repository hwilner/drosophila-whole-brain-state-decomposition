# Current Results and Discussion

## Current Result

This project has completed two separate observational routes. They answer different questions and are kept separate rather than combined.

One route examined the relationship between neural components and motion-related signals. In the eligible animals, it supported a limited association that was present when assessed on observations not used to fit the analysis. Evidence involving annotation-related signals was heterogeneous. Adding annotation-related signals to motion-related signals was not consistently better than using motion-related signals alone.

The other route used a separate observational prediction design. It was non-supportive overall for its own question: its held-out improvement relative to the fixed baseline had mixed directions. It therefore does not provide consistent support within that route.

No cross-route synthesis or pooling was run. No further data-dependent work was run for this public release. Deferred work includes data acquisition and handling, candidate-material assessment, data-dependent preprocessing, empirical state-decomposition work, and artifact-based visualizations.

## Interpretation

The supportive finding is narrow. Within the stated route and eligible animals, motion-related signals were associated with neural components in a held-out assessment. A held-out assessment asks whether a pattern fitted on one set of observations also appears in a separate set. This makes the result more useful as a descriptive, predictive observation, but it does not explain why the signals are related.

The mixed annotation-related findings and the lack of uniform improvement from combined predictors mean that this route does not offer one consistently stronger combined account. The separate route should be read on its own terms: its overall non-supportive result does not cancel or confirm the first route, because the routes were designed separately and were not pooled.

## What the Result Does Not Show

The findings do not show that motion causes changes in brain activity, that brain activity causes motion, or that a biological mechanism has been identified. They do not establish a common neural state across animals, learning, memory, individual-neuron effects, raw-voxel effects, or a relationship beyond the specific observational settings described here.

The findings also do not show that annotation-related signals are never useful, or that combined predictors are always worse. They show only that the available annotation-related evidence was heterogeneous and that combined predictors were not uniformly better in the completed route. The non-supportive second route does not establish absence of every possible relationship; it does not provide consistent support for its own predictive question under its fixed evaluation.

Neither route is a replication of the other, and the results do not support a cross-system conclusion. They should not be merged into a single estimate or treated as evidence for a shared explanation.

## Discussion and Future Testing

Future work would need project-owner approval and an appropriate governed data boundary before any data-dependent step. It could ask whether the component–motion association remains present when eligibility rules, predictors, and evaluation criteria are stated before examining outcomes. It could ask whether clearly defined annotation-related signals add reliable held-out value beyond motion-related signals under a route-specific plan. It could also ask whether the separate prediction design shows a consistent held-out pattern relative to a fixed baseline when evaluated under its own pre-specified conditions. A causal question would require a separately approved experimental design rather than another observational comparison.

Any future evaluation should keep the routes separate unless an owner-approved plan explicitly justifies another treatment. It should state the intended inference, eligibility rules, predictors, baseline, held-out partition, and success criteria before results are inspected. Choices made while fitting or selecting a model should be contained within the validation process. Reports should preserve supportive, non-supportive, and mixed outcomes rather than selecting only favorable results, and should retain the distinction between prediction, association, and causation.

No future test is run or implied by this document.
